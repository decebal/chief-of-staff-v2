# Goals that work together

Use the user's confirmed direction, not a generic growth prescription. A strategic review may use saved evidence; clearly mark stale or missing measurements and ask before expanding source scope.

## Goal map

For each goal, record: who benefits, outcome/finish line, owner, target date, measurable KPI, supporting goals, competing goals, capacity needed, and important assumptions. A goal may support another through reusable capability, trust, revenue, delivery quality, distribution, or reduced dependence on one person. Name the actual link and whether it is confirmed or only a hypothesis.

Watch for conflicting incentives: growth that overloads delivery, delivery that prevents acquisition, automation that adds more maintenance than it removes, and short-term wins that undermine a long-term asset. Obligations and safety do not become optional because they lack a strategic label.

## Work backward

1. Define the outcome and its observable acceptance criteria.
2. Identify the last prerequisite needed before the finish line.
3. Walk backward through dependencies, approval windows, and lead times.
4. Place achievable deliverables into weeks; show this week's execution first.
5. Assign proposed owners and dates explicitly until confirmed. Record blockers and what counts as done. A week is a planning window, not a hard deadline.
6. Compare the user's remaining capacity with their work, not the whole team's combined effort. Offer one concrete trade-off when needed.

Keep KPIs distinct from deliverables. “Publish five articles” is output; “generate five qualified opportunities” is an outcome, if that is the confirmed goal. Do not invent attribution or treat registrations as paid customers. Store numerator, denominator or unit where needed, reporting window, and evidence date. Preserve nulls for unknowns.

## Weekly steering review

Ask which goal gained evidence, which assumption weakened, and what has become the bottleneck. Recommend maintain, sequence differently, reduce scope, test, delegate, or stop. Say what new evidence would change the recommendation. Keep a dated decision record; a strategic hypothesis is not proof of completion.

Monthly, compare outcomes against the original baseline and target, not just the number of tasks finished. Keep past completed weeks in history but remove them from the default active execution view. Do not silently carry overdue work forward or erase it.

Return a short recommendation and the revised proposed plan. Do not overwrite confirmed goals or introduce a new organizational direction without user approval.
