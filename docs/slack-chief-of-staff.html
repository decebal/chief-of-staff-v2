1 · Channel post

I set up a chief of staff for myself: two AI agents and a small CLI. Every morning it tells me who is waiting on me.

What it does:

  • Tactical brain, weekday mornings: reads my calendar, inbox, Jira, task tracker and GitHub, and returns at most five things to do today, each with its source. Reviews that someone else is blocked on rank first.
  • Strategic brain, Fridays: checks the week's work against my written goals for the quarter. It says which goals moved, which work served none of them, and which decisions only I can take.
  • Chief of staff: I ask a question ("release review or the crypto fix today?"). It sends it to one or both brains and merges their answers into one recommendation.

Why I think everyone would want one:

  • The first thing I see is who is blocked on me, not whatever arrived last.
  • It lists what it could not read. When my calendar connection dropped, the brief said "calendar not read" instead of showing me an empty day.
  • Once a week it compares what I did with what I said mattered. That gap is hard to see from inside the week.
  • It is read-only. It sends nothing and edits nothing; it recommends and I act.
  • It is cheap: about $0.30 for a daily brief, about $1.50 for a question that needs both brains.

2 · Thread reply: how to set up your own

If you want your own, in this order:

  1. Write your goals down first. Three bets for the quarter, a paragraph each: the outcome, where the work lives, what is out of scope. Without this the strategic half can only guess.
  2. Split the horizons. One prompt for today, one for the quarter. A single "help me prioritise" prompt mixes them and does neither well.
  3. Connect only what you actually read. Calendar, mail, your tracker. Every extra connector adds context the model has to load on each run.
  4. Keep it read-only. Let it suggest the reply or the ticket change; you send it.
  5. Require a source on every item, and a "not read" list. A failed connection should show up as a gap, never as an empty inbox.
  6. Rank by who is blocked on you, then by what today's meetings need.
  7. Ask for confidence, not hours. "Understood / will shift / unknown" is more honest than a time estimate.

Three ways to run it:

  • No code: a Claude Project with your goals doc as project knowledge, your connectors switched on, and two saved prompts (daily brief, weekly review).
  • Claude Code: three agent files in ~/.claude/agents/ (a router, a tactician, a strategist). Happy to share mine.
  • Scheduled: mine is a small Rust CLI that runs both on a timer. Ask and I'll walk you through it.
